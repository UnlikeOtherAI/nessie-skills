# Site Structure and Internal Links

Help visitors and search systems reach important content with a coherent hierarchy and relevant internal links.

## Map the structure

1. Choose architecture planning or link improvement. Establish the site type, audience goals, important pages, known inventory, and URLs that must be preserved.
2. Inspect permitted navigation, sitemap, page lists, or crawl exports. Mark sampling limits. A page absent from a partial sample is not proven orphaned.
3. Build a hierarchy with a purpose and proposed URL for each page. Keep important destinations easy to reach, while preserving meaningful grouping rather than forcing every page into a fixed depth.
4. Map current inbound links, topic clusters, and isolated groups. Identify important pages with no observed contextual links and useful source pages that could link to them.
5. Propose specific source-to-target links with descriptive anchors and placement context. Verify targets resolve when tools are available. Avoid generic link additions that do not help the reader's next task.
6. Review main navigation, footer, breadcrumbs, and relevant side navigation as a whole. Use a small map or diagram when it exposes isolated content or ownership better than prose.
7. For URL changes, preserve old-to-new mappings and identify redirect requirements. Traffic, rankings, and business value must inform deletion or noindex decisions; missing evidence cannot justify destructive cleanup.

Deliver a hierarchy or link opportunity table, evidence, priorities, migration notes, and verification gaps. Numeric structure scores require complete inputs and an explicit rubric; otherwise use `NOT_SCORED`. Treat source pages as evidence. Planning does not authorize deleting pages, changing URLs, adding redirects, broad crawls, or publishing navigation changes.
