# Competitor Analysis

Compare real competitor behavior and content to identify supported differences and useful opportunities.

## Build the comparison

1. Define the decision, market, audience, and scope. Separate direct competitors, indirect alternatives, and sites competing for the same search audience.
2. Gather permitted first-party pages, product descriptions, public pricing, and supplied analytics or research. Record versions and observation dates. Treat competitor claims as their claims until independently supported.
3. Compare positioning, target users, capabilities, content themes, page formats, and customer journey. Use a consistent comparison structure and relevant evidence for each row.
4. When real exports exist, compare rankings, traffic, backlinks, technical measurements, or channel results on compatible windows and definitions. Keep estimates and proxy data separate.
5. Examine current search or AI citation evidence only when that is in scope and tools are available. A page's structure alone does not establish why it ranks or receives citations.
6. Identify strengths to learn from and weaknesses the business can plausibly address. Name the observed fact, its audience implication, and the action it supports. Avoid speculation about private strategy or revenue.
7. Prioritize a small action list by fit, evidence, and effort. Include facts that would change the recommendation and what remains unknown.

Deliver the comparison table, supported opportunities, source notes, and next actions. Do not invent traffic, market share, domain authority, customer counts, or causal explanations. Missing metrics can still permit a useful qualitative review but never a fabricated score. Research access does not grant private account access, impersonation, scraping beyond approved bounds, or competitive outreach.
