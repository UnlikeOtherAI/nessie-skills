# Discovery Questionnaire

Gather facts and decisions from the person who has them with a focused questionnaire they can answer asynchronously or in a meeting.

## Design the questions

1. Use the known context to identify the recipient's role, what they know, and the decision the user needs to make. Ask only for missing information that changes the questionnaire.
2. Map each needed fact or decision to a question. Remove questions the available documents already answer. Put important questions first because the recipient may provide only a partial response.
3. Begin with a short purpose, enough context for someone outside the conversation, how answers will be used, and a supplied deadline if one exists. Do not invent a recipient, deadline, or commitment.
4. Group related questions when the questionnaire is long. Ask one idea at a time. Offer options where they clarify a real choice, with space for an explanation or an unknown answer.
5. Include answer space under each question. Add a brief reason only where the question might otherwise receive an unhelpful answer. Invite the recipient to distinguish facts, estimates, and uncertainty.
6. End with an opportunity to identify something important that the questionnaire missed. Check that every required decision has coverage and that the expected effort is proportionate.

Deliver the questionnaire in the requested format and a short note about the decisions its answers will support. Drafting does not authorize sending it, scheduling a meeting, or writing into another team's document. Avoid requesting sensitive personal information unless the authorized task requires it and a suitable secure channel exists.
