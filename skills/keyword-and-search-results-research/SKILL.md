# Keyword and Search Results Research

Find search topics worth addressing by comparing current result patterns, audience intent, and trustworthy demand data.

## Research the opportunities

1. Confirm the product, audience, business goal, geography, language, and search engine. Use the supplied brief and permitted site or analytics evidence.
2. Build a bounded seed list from the audience's actual tasks, problems, solutions, and vocabulary. Expand related questions and variations using research rather than fixed trigger-word rules.
3. Inspect current or supplied search-result snapshots. Record query, engine, locale, device, source, and observation time. Capture relevant result features and representative ranking pages.
4. Infer intent from the whole observed result and audience context. Compare page formats, depth, freshness, useful differentiators, and unanswered needs. Treat intent as a model judgment backed by evidence.
5. Use real exports or approved tools for volume, difficulty, ranking, and cost metrics. Label each measured, user-provided, calculated, estimated, proxy, or unknown. Preserve disagreement between tools and periods.
6. Cluster topics around a coherent audience task and a suitable page. Identify where new content would add value and where an existing page should be improved instead of duplicated.
7. Prioritize by relevance, achievable effort, and business value. A numeric opportunity score requires its declared formula and complete compatible inputs. Missing decision-critical metrics mean `NOT_SCORED`, not invented volume or difficulty.

Deliver a query/cluster table, observed result evidence, suggested page format or outline, and prioritized opportunities with uncertainty. Do not promise rankings or AI citations. If live search is unavailable, clearly label the supplied snapshot's date and coverage. Research does not authorize automated crawling, new tool installation, or publishing pages.
