# Unslop

Make writing clear, specific, and natural while preserving its meaning, useful detail, uncertainty, and the person's voice.

## Edit the message

1. Identify the reader's question and the decision or action the answer supports. Lead with that answer. Keep the explanation needed to assess it.
2. Remove filler, praise, hype, canned chatbot phrases, repeated summaries, and claims that describe importance without explaining what happened. Replace vague attributions with named evidence or remove the claim.
3. Use plain words and concrete verbs. Say who does what. Keep one name for each concept. Explain necessary technical terms where they first matter.
4. Split sentences that require a second reading. Restore missing articles, verbs, and context when compressed fragments become hard to decode. A short answer can still be unclear.
5. Keep numbers with their units, evidence, and relevant limits. Preserve disagreement and uncertainty. Do not make a cautious claim sound certain merely to shorten it.
6. Use paragraphs for connected ideas. Use lists for real sequences or comparisons. Use headings and bold text only when they help the reader navigate substantial material. Remove decorative symbols and rhetorical punctuation.
7. Read the result as the intended reader. Each sentence should answer a question, explain evidence, identify a constraint, or name the next action. Remove sentences that do none of these.

## Preserve the interaction

Match the user's language and level of detail. Keep quotations accurate and preserve their attribution. Do not edit code, identifiers, legal terms, or quoted material as if they were ordinary prose.

For an acknowledgment or verified success, a reaction or an emoji-only reply can be complete. Use an existing permitted reaction action when appropriate. Add text when the person needs context, a blocker, a question, or a next step. An emoji is not evidence that an unverified action succeeded.

Deliver the revised text directly unless the user asked for an explanation of the edits. Substantial analysis still needs substantial detail. This skill does not impose a short answer on every task.
