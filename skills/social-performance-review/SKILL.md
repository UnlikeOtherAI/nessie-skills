# Social Performance Review

Compare social results on consistent definitions and periods to identify supported lessons and bounded next experiments.

## Analyze the evidence

1. Define the channels, accounts, period, objective, and comparison baseline. Use authorized dashboards or supplied exports, and record coverage, timezone, and observation date.
2. Match rows to actual published content when possible. Exclude plans or draft slots from outcome totals. Mark missing publication or content-version evidence as a coverage gap.
3. Define each metric. Engagements divided by reach, impressions, or followers are different rates. Keep the denominator consistent across periods and disclose any definition change.
4. Separate organic, boosted, and paid results. Compare compatible periods and cohorts. Do not splice an organic trend into a paid one or sum overlapping audience reach as unique people.
5. Calculate per-post distributions and medians as useful, retaining the actual counts and sample size. Explain outliers rather than letting one exceptional post define the baseline.
6. Compare outcomes with declared goals and prior evidence. Explain what changed, then label possible causes as hypotheses. Posting-hour lore and platform explanations are not measured causes.
7. Suggest keep, stop, or try actions only when coverage and the measurement window support them. Incomplete data yields provisional hypotheses and a plan to obtain the missing evidence.

Deliver channel summaries, metric definitions, representative posts, evidence-backed lessons, and next experiment proposals. Earned media value, if requested and supported by a source-dated formula, stays an estimated communication figure separate from revenue and organic trends. No invented score or causal lift. Review does not authorize account changes, spend changes, publishing, or monitoring schedules.
