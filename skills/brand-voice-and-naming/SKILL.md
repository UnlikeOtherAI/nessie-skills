# Brand Voice and Naming

Define a usable voice and consistent naming rules from a brand's own material and approved messages.

## Extract and test the rules

1. Gather representative first-party copy, current message priorities, audience context, and known naming decisions. Use competitor examples only for comparison, never as evidence of this brand's voice.
2. Identify the register and the tone choices that vary by situation, such as formal versus conversational or technical versus introductory. Explain what each choice changes in actual sentences.
3. Write a compact voice card with preferred vocabulary, recurring mistakes to avoid, and a few before/after examples from the supplied material. Preserve the useful meaning and the audience's language.
4. Define product, feature, and plan names: capitalization, article use, generic versus branded references, abbreviations, and versions. List approved and deprecated terms with their evidence or decision owner.
5. Check examples against current claims and product stage. A strong voice cannot make an unsupported comparison true. Mark such claims `[needs source]` rather than embedding them in reusable examples.
6. Compare rules with existing approved guidance. Identify conflicts and propose a clear resolution instead of silently creating a competing rulebook.
7. Test the card on several real contexts, such as an introduction, help reply, and product description. Show where the tone changes while names and factual promises remain consistent.

Deliver the voice card, naming table, worked examples, and open decisions. Trademark or legal status requires appropriate current evidence and review; do not certify a name as available. Research and document writes follow existing permissions. This workflow does not authorize publishing a new brand identity or updating all live copy.
